1

Cleanse

Wash your face thoroughly with a mild, pH-balanced cleanser to remove makeup, oil, and dirt.

2

Pat Dry

Gently pat your skin dry with a clean towel. Avoid rubbing, which can cause micro-irritation.

3

Wait

Leave your skin bare—apply no serums, moisturizers, or treatments. Wait for exactly 30 minutes.

4

Observe

Examine your skin in a mirror and take note of any shine, tightness, or areas of redness.

The
Bare-Face Test

The gold standard of skin analysis. Minimalist, scientific, and revealing. Discover how your skin behaves when left to its own devices.

Observe Your Results

Match your findings from the Bare-Face test below.

Outcome A
Tight & Flaky

If your skin feels parched or displays dry patches, you likely have Dry Skin.

Outcome B
All-Over Shine

If your forehead, nose, and cheeks all show a noticeable sheen, you have Oily Skin.

Outcome C
T-Zone Only

If shine is limited to your forehead and nose, but cheeks feel dry, you have Combination Skin.

Outcome D
Comfortable

If your skin feels supple and looks clear with no major concerns, you have Normal Skin.

Outcome E
Irritated & Red

If the test makes your skin sting, itch, or turn red, you have Sensitive Skin.

The Blotting Paper Test

A faster alternative for daytime assessment. This method focuses solely on sebum production patterns.

1

Press a clean blotting paper onto different areas of your face (Forehead, Nose, Cheeks, Chin).

2

Hold the paper up to a light source to see how much oil it absorbed from each zone.

Reading the Paper

  • Heavily soaked Oily
  • Cheeks clear, T-zone oily Combination
  • Little to no oil Dry
  • Minimal oil evenly spread Normal

Why Knowing Your Skin Type Matters

Skin care is not a one-size-fits-all journey. Using products designed for dry skin on an oily complexion can lead to clogged pores and breakouts, while harsh oily-skin treatments can damage a dry skin barrier. When you know your type, you invest in results, not just rituals.

Pro Editorial Tip

Remember that skin type can shift with age, climate, and hormonal changes. We recommend performing the Bare-Face test at least twice a year—once in the summer and once in the winter—to ensure your routine remains perfectly aligned with your skin's current needs.
